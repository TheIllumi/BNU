## Human Computer Interaction

### What is HCI?

HCI is about working in quick iterations so we can figure out problems sooner rather than later.

The core cycle of HCI involves three stages:

- **Design** - Plan and conceptualize the interaction
- **Implementation** - Build the design
- **Evaluation** - Assess the result and feed findings back into design

This cycle repeats continuously to catch and resolve problems as early as possible.

### Key Questions HCI Addresses

- What is HCI?
- What makes a design a good design?
- What makes it a bad one?
- What is one factor which is central to all interaction design?

## User Centered Design (UCD)

The central factor in all interaction design is the **user**.

User Centered Design means:

- Design with people in mind; people's tasks, goals, and values should drive your development
- Work with users throughout the process
- Assess decisions from the users' point of view, their work, and their environment
- Pay attention to people's abilities and situations

---

## What is a Good User Experience?

A good UX is characterized by the following qualities:

- Useful
- Easy to Learn
- Fun
- Helpful
- Connected
- Satisfying
- Accessible
- Attractive
- Delightful

---

## What is a Bad User Experience?

A bad UX is characterized by the following qualities:

- Stressful
- Inefficient
- Confusing
- Distracting
- Ugly
- Tedious
- Inconsiderate
- Frustrating
- Condescending

## Why Does UX Matter?

UX matters because success happens at the intersection of:

- What users want to do
- What you want users to do

When these overlap, you achieve **Experience Success**, which leads to:

- Meeting product goals
- Repeat usage
- Users recommending the product to others

## The UX as a Whole

UX is the experience people have when they interact with your product. This includes every touchpoint:

- Using the product
- Choosing it
- Acquiring it
- Learning to use it
- Fixing the product
- Upgrading the product

## Interaction Design

### What is Interaction Design?

Interaction Design is the practice of developing interactive products that are:

- Usable
- Easy to learn
- Effective to use
- Enjoyable from a user experience standpoint

### Goal of Interaction Design

- Develop usable products
- Usability means easy to learn, effective to use, and providing an enjoyable experience
- Involve users in the design process

---

## Which Kind of Design?

There are many terms used to emphasize what is being designed:

- User interface design
- Software design
- User-centered design
- Product design
- Web design
- Experience design (UX)

**Interaction design is the umbrella term** covering all of these aspects. It is fundamental to all disciplines, fields, and approaches concerned with researching and designing computer-based systems for people.

## What Do Professionals Do in the ID Business?

- **Interaction Designers** - people involved in the design of all the interactive aspects of a product
- **Usability Engineers** - people who focus on evaluating products, using usability methods and principles
- **Web Designers** - people who develop and create the visual design of websites, such as layouts

## UX in the Light of Interaction Design

### User Experience

- How a product behaves and is used by people in the real world
- The way people feel about it and their pleasure and satisfaction when using it, looking at it, holding it, and opening or closing it

> "Every product that is used by someone has a user experience: newspapers, ketchup bottles, reclining armchairs, cardigan sweaters." - Garrett, 2010

> "Great design is the iteration of good design."

**Important distinction:** You cannot design a user experience, you can only **design for** a user experience.

---

## Usability vs. User Experience

### Definition of Usability

> "Usability is the extent to which a product can be used by specified users to achieve specified goals with effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction in a specified context of use." - DIN EN ISO 9241-11

### Is Usability Enough?

Fulfilling the usability definition leads to:

- Products that do not prevent users from performing their tasks
- Products that can be used effectively and efficiently
- Products that do not annoy users

But is this really sufficient to build great products that users enjoy to use? **No.**

The sink analogy illustrates this: all three variants of a sink (old and rusty, standard clean, premium designer) are equally "usable" in the functional sense, but the user experience they provide is vastly different. Usability is necessary but not sufficient.

### User Experience is More Than Just Usability

User Experience is influenced by a wide range of factors:

- Actual product-related factors such as features and quality attributes
- Brand and image
- Packaging
- Marketing
- And many more

---

## User Experience - Formally Defined

> "User experience includes all aspects of the user's experience when interacting with the product, service, environment or facility. It is a consequence of the presentation, functionality, system performance, interactive behavior, and assistive capabilities of the interactive system. It includes all aspects of usability and desirability of a product, system or service from the user's perspective."

---

## User Centered Design - Relationships

The relationship between UCD, Usability Engineering, and User Experience:

- **Usability** is concerned with the period **during usage**
- **User Experience** extends beyond this, covering before and after usage as well
- **Usability Engineering** spans from expectation (before) to actual experience (after), measured and refined
- **User Centered Design** is the overarching process that encompasses all of the above

In summary: Usability is a subset of User Experience, and both are addressed through User Centered Design.

## Usability & User Experience Goals

### Usability Goals

- Effective to use
- Efficient to use
- Safe to use
- Have good utility
- Easy to learn
- Easy to remember how to use

### User Experience Goals

**Desirable Aspects:**
- Satisfying
- Helpful
- Fun
- Enjoyable
- Motivating
- Provocative
- Engaging
- Challenging
- Surprising
- Pleasurable
- Enhancing sociability
- Rewarding
- Exciting
- Supporting creativity
- Emotionally fulfilling
- Entertaining
- Cognitively stimulating

**Undesirable Aspects:**
- Boring
- Unpleasant
- Frustrating
- Patronizing
- Making one feel guilty
- Making one feel stupid
- Annoying
- Cutesy
- Childish
- Gimmicky

### Balancing Usability and User Experience Goals

- Selecting terms to convey a person's feelings and emotions can help designers understand the multifaceted nature of the user experience
- Usability goals are more objective and easier to measure (e.g., task completion time, error rate)
- User experience goals are more subjective and harder to measure (e.g., how fun or emotionally fulfilling something feels)
- There can be trade-offs between the two, e.g., can a product be both fun and safe?

## How Has Human-Computer Interaction Changed Over Time?

**Core question:** How has the way humans interact with computers changed over time, and why does that matter for UX/UXE today?

---

## Timeline of HCI Paradigms

### Timeline 1 - Early Computing / Batch Processing

- Model: Submit -> Wait -> Get output
- Example: Punch cards and mainframes
- UX Issues:
  - No real-time feedback
  - No usability concept yet
  - No interaction at all

---

### Timeline 2 - Command Line Interaction (CLI)

- Model: Text commands
- Examples: DOS, Linux terminal
- UX Issues:
  - Expert users only
  - Memorization required
  - Low discoverability
  - Cognitive load is high
  - Learnability is low

---

### Timeline 3 - GUI / WIMP Paradigm

- Model: Graphical User Interface; Windows, Icons, Menus, Pointer
- Examples: Windows, MacOS, Desktop applications
- UX Impact:
  - Visual interaction replaces memorization
  - Discoverability increases
  - Affordances begin to matter
  - Direct manipulation begins
  - Heuristics and usability principles emerge

---

### Timeline 4 - Web & Hyperlink Paradigm

- Model: Navigation-based interaction
- Examples: Websites and browsers
- UX Impact:
  - Shift from applications to information spaces
  - Information architecture becomes critical
  - Navigation matters
  - Consistency becomes key

---

### Timeline 5 - Touch & Mobile Paradigm

- Model: Direct touch manipulation
- Examples: Smartphones and tablets
- UX Consequences:
  - Shift from pointer to finger
  - Target size matters
  - Gesture discoverability issues (e.g., swipe left to archive in Gmail)
  - Context-aware UX becomes important
- Context-Aware Dimensions:
  - **Location** - map recommendations based on where you are
  - **Time** - apps show breakfast items in the morning
  - **Behavior** - most recently used apps shown first
  - **Orientation** - portrait vs landscape layouts

---

### Timeline 6 - Voice & Conversational Interfaces

- Model: Natural language interaction
- Examples: Alexa, Siri, chatbots
- UX Challenges:
  - The interface disappears
  - No visual affordance; users do not know what actions are possible
  - Feedback is invisible (e.g., a voice assistant pauses for 3 seconds with no visible cue)
  - Error recovery is harder; no way of going back

---

### Timeline 7 - AI & Adaptive Interfaces (Current Shift)

- Model: System adapts to the user
- Examples: ChatGPT, AI copilots, recommendation systems
- UX Implications:
  - User no longer fully controls the flow
  - Trust becomes key
  - Transparency matters
  - Explainability is needed
  - Ethical UX is required

---

## Timeline Summary

Each paradigm shift changed:

- How users think
- What designers must optimize
- What engineers must implement

**UX rules are not static - they evolve with interaction paradigms.**

## Summary

The key concepts from this lecture are:

- HCI is about designing for people through iterative design, implementation, and evaluation
- User Centered Design puts users' tasks, goals, and values at the core of development
- Good UX is useful, helpful, accessible, satisfying, and delightful; bad UX is frustrating, confusing, and stressful
- UX matters because it drives product success, repeat usage, and word-of-mouth
- UX encompasses the full lifecycle of a product, not just active usage
- Interaction Design is the umbrella field that covers UI design, UX design, web design, and more
- Usability is a necessary but not sufficient condition for good UX
- UX formally includes all aspects of usability and desirability across the full interaction context
- The history of HCI spans batch processing, CLI, GUI, Web, Mobile, Voice, and now AI paradigms
- Each paradigm shift redefines what good design means
